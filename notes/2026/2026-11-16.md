## Key Points

- Culture of Christ vs. church: Norms are learned through groups and modeling, with examples from personal experience in Saudi Arabia and Utah
- Terminal values: End goals like happiness, family security, and peace that recur across cultures
- Instrumental values: Means to achieve terminal values, such as ambition, self-control, and honesty
- Value quadrants: Schwartz's model categorizes societies into openness to change, self-enhancement, conservation, and self-transcendence
- Cultural examples: Nordic countries show openness to change despite traditions; Japanese culture reflects conservation through risk aversion
- Power distance: U.S. scores moderately low (40) compared to Saudi Arabia (high power distance)
- Uncertainty avoidance: High in cultures that prefer clear rules over ambiguity, influencing organizational behavior
- Short-term vs. long-term orientation: Short-term leads to fatalism and lower productivity; long-term supports innovation and economic growth
- Innovation and reliability: Often opposing priorities requiring trade-offs in organizations
- Cultural identity: Strong identity drives deep compliance with traditions rather than mere rule-following
- Group norms: Individuals may conform to group expectations but retain personal values when alone
- Exam focus: Final exam applies units one, two, three concepts to scenarios, emphasizing understanding over quantity
