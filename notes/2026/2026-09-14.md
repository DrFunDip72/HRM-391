## Summary

The lecture covers organizational survival through environmental fit, using examples like Kodak and Blockbuster to illustrate failure from misalignment. It emphasizes scanning both task and general environments, identifying critical issues, and mapping dependencies to avoid premature solutions. The course integrates practical exercises like exit tickets, PESTLE analysis, and environmental characterization to reinforce understanding.

## Key Points

- Concept — Environmental fit: Critical for organizational survival; incompatibility leads to shutdown (e.g., Kodak, Blockbuster).
- Concept — Task environment: Immediate competitors, suppliers, and stakeholders affecting daily operations.
- Concept — General environment: Broader factors like technology, regulations (PESTLE framework: Political, Economic, Social, Technological, Legal, Environmental).
- Concept — Environmental characterization: Labeling business environment as constrained (labor issues), complex (interwoven parts), or change-driven (rapid shifts).
- Concept — Dependency matrices: Categorize issues by criticality (low/medium/high) and replaceability to prioritize responses.
- Concept — PESTLE analysis: Identify how external factors impact organizational fit (e.g., tariffs, AI, protests against Chick-fil-A).
- Concept — Scanning the environment: Identify critical issues (e.g., backlog, competitors) and their impact before implementing solutions.
- Concept — Labor market constraints: High retention (6-5 years) vs. massive turnover in stores, affecting brand alignment and operational stability.
- Big takeaway — The lecture covers organizational survival through environmental fit, using examples like Kodak and Blockbuster to illustrate failure from misalignment.
