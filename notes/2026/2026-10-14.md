## Key Points

- Group and team processes are vital for daily life and careers, involving interactions with coworkers, family, and communities.
- Tuckman's four stages of group development (forming, storming, norming, performing) with a fifth stage (breaking up or disbanding) explain team evolution.
- Cohesion strengthens group norms and behaviors, enabling mutual support and defense while penalizing outliers who deviate from established norms.
- Psychological safety allows risk-taking without fear of punishment, critical for accurate decision-making and learning, as seen in the space shuttle O-Ring incident.
- Social architecture emerges from group interactions, creating norms and expectations that influence behavior and cohesion.
- Teams require interdependence and coordinated roles to achieve shared goals, unlike groups which may lack coordination despite shared location or management.
- Process gains occur when contributions improve group outcomes, while process losses happen when contributions harm performance, even if results seem positive.
- Social loping involves intentionally reducing effort to avoid participation, distinct from passive non-participation, and can lead to negative impacts on team dynamics.
- Breaking bread is an assignment to coordinate service project planning through intentional group design.
