Key concepts include power as relational and issue-specific, with five sources: legitimate, reward, coercive, expert, and referent power. ## Key Points

- Unit 3 focuses on power and influence after reviewing groups, teams, perception, and motivation.
- Power is relational and issue-specific, influenced by knowledge, titles, timelines, and perceptions.
- The five sources of power: legitimate (formal authority), reward (control over benefits), coercive (punishment), expert (specialized knowledge), and referent (trust and credibility).
- In the Northstar Software case, Maria controls launch timelines and final recommendations, Eli manages critical vulnerability knowledge, Rachel holds a scarce $180,000 contingency budget, and Sam influences team commitment.
- Power shifts with dependencies: high importance and low alternatives increase power, while more alternatives decrease it.
- Effective influence strategies include understanding response types (compliance vs. commitment) and leveraging relationships for time or resources.
- Dependency determines influence strategy: critical dependencies require commitment, while low-stakes tasks may use compliance.
- Real-world examples include project managers influencing analysts with tight deadlines and negotiating time with supervisors.
- Big takeaway — This study guide covers Unit 3 on power and influence, following units on groups, teams, perception, and motivation.
