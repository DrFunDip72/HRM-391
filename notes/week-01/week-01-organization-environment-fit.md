# Week 1 — Organization–Environment Fit Primer

## Big Idea

Organizations depend on their environments for customers, resources, labor, capital, information, legitimacy, permission to operate, partnerships, and other things they need to survive and perform.

The central idea this week is **fit**:

> An organizational practice is not inherently effective. Its value depends on what the situation requires.

**Organization–environment fit** = the match between what the relevant environment requires and what the organization is doing.

There is no universally "good" practice — only practices that fit (or don't fit) a given environment.

---

## 1. Two Parts of the Environment

### Task / Immediate Environment

External actors or conditions with which the organization has **direct exchange, dependence, competition, oversight, or interaction**.

Examples:
- Customers
- Suppliers
- Competitors
- Labor pools
- Regulators
- Funders
- Partners
- Owners
- Distributors
- Platforms
- Communities

### General Environment

Broader conditions affecting the organization — the backdrop the task environment sits inside.

Scanning tool: **PESTEL**
| Letter | Category |
|---|---|
| P | Political |
| E | Economic |
| S | Social / Demographic |
| T | Technological |
| E | Environmental / Ecological |
| L | Legal / Regulatory |

> PESTEL is a **scanning tool, not the whole analysis** — it surfaces candidates for attention, it doesn't tell you what matters or why.

---

## 2. Look for Dependencies

A **dependency** exists when the organization relies on something outside itself that it does not fully control.

Diagnostic questions to ask of any dependency:
1. What do we need?
2. From whom?
3. How important is it?
4. How easily can it be replaced?
5. What happens if its **price, availability, quality, timing, permission, or demand** changes?

---

## 3. Characterize the Environment

Four conditions to assess:

| Condition | Question |
|---|---|
| **Change** | How quickly and significantly are relevant conditions shifting? |
| **Complexity** | How many different and interconnected factors must the organization understand? |
| **Uncertainty** | How difficult is it to predict what will happen, what it means, or what response will work? |
| **Resource Constraints** | How scarce, costly, concentrated, restricted, or unreliable are important resources or capabilities? |

> **Remember:** fast change does not necessarily mean high uncertainty. The two are independent dimensions — something can change quickly and predictably (e.g., a scheduled regulatory phase-in), or change slowly yet remain highly uncertain.

---

## 4. Signal ≠ Demand ≠ Solution

Three distinct things that are easy to collapse into one — keep them separate.

| Term | Definition |
|---|---|
| **External signal** | Something in the environment has changed. |
| **Organizational demand** | What the organization now needs to be able to do or deliver. |
| **Possible solution** | One way the organization might respond. |

### Worked Example

- **Signal:** A new certification requirement begins in nine months.
- **Demand:** The organization must be able to verify compliance before the requirement takes effect.
- **Solution:** Change suppliers or purchase new compliance software.

> **Do not jump from the signal directly to a solution.** Skipping the "demand" step means you can pick a solution that solves the wrong problem.

---

## 5. The Week 1 Diagnostic Sequence

**SCAN → MAP → CHARACTERIZE → DEMANDS → FIT → TRACE INWARD**

| Step | What you do |
|---|---|
| **SCAN** | Notice relevant external evidence. |
| **MAP** | Identify task/immediate actors, broader conditions, and important dependencies. |
| **CHARACTERIZE** | Describe change, complexity, uncertainty, and resource constraints. |
| **DEMANDS** | Determine what the organization must be able to do or deliver. |
| **FIT** | Compare those demands with what the organization currently does and is capable of doing. |
| **TRACE INWARD** | Identify which parts of the organization appear most involved. |

> **For now, concentrate on understanding the situation before recommending changes.** This diagnostic is descriptive/analytical, not yet prescriptive — jumping to solutions before completing FIT and TRACE INWARD undermines the whole exercise.

---

## Quick-Reference Summary

- Fit, not universal best practice, determines whether something works.
- Environment = **Task/Immediate** (direct interaction) + **General** (PESTEL backdrop).
- A **dependency** = reliance on something you don't control — interrogate it on importance, replaceability, and what happens if it changes.
- Environments are described along four **independent** axes: change, complexity, uncertainty, resource constraints.
- Keep **signal → demand → solution** in strict order; never skip straight from signal to solution.
- Run the diagnostic in order: **SCAN → MAP → CHARACTERIZE → DEMANDS → FIT → TRACE INWARD**.

## Study Questions

1. For your default organization, list 3–5 task/immediate environment actors and 2–3 general environment (PESTEL) factors currently relevant to it.
2. Pick one dependency your default organization has. Walk it through all five dependency questions.
3. Rate your default organization's environment on change, complexity, uncertainty, and resource constraints (low/med/high each) — and justify why change and uncertainty aren't necessarily linked in this case.
4. Take a real signal you've noticed in your default organization and write out its signal → demand → solution chain without skipping steps.
5. Run the full SCAN → MAP → CHARACTERIZE → DEMANDS → FIT → TRACE INWARD sequence on your default organization at a high level.
